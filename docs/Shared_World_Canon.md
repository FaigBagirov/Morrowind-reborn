# Shared World Canon

**This file is attached to both projects: Morrowind and Skyrim.**

It contains everything that is true of the world regardless of which game is being converted. Anything specific to one game lives in that game's canon file.

| Document | Scope |
| --- | --- |
| **Shared World Canon** (this file) | The world, the species, the rules of the setting |
| *Morrowind Conversion Canon* | Vvardenfell: Tribunal, Dagoth Ur, Corprus, the magic gate, visuals |
| *Skyrim Conversion Canon* | Skyrim: the Voice, dragons, Alduin, the archive |
| *Morrowind Conversion Architecture* | Method and safety rules for OpenMW |
| *Morrowind Next-Gen Installation Guide* | What to install (Morrowind) |

## Ownership rule `SETTLED`

**This file is edited only in the Morrowind project, then re-uploaded to the Skyrim project.** One owner, one edit point. Two copies that both accept edits will diverge, and the divergence will not be noticed until it contradicts something already written.

# Part 0 — The Hard Boundary `SETTLED`

**The setting explains mechanisms. The setting does not explain the origin of the world, the nature of the soul, or what happens after death.**

This is not a stylistic preference and it is not negotiable. It applies to both games and to every line written for either.

**1. Nobody in the setting made the world, and nobody in the setting is great.** The Zenar and the Zenad are terraformers: tenants who reworked a house they did not raise. Any phrasing that reads as "the world is made of them", "they built the planet", or that assigns them greatness or majesty, is wrong. Greatness is not theirs to be assigned. They were strong, their strength was barely enough for one world, and it nearly finished them.

**2. The soul gets no technical description.** Under the Rename Test (Part 9), "soul" is a mortal's word for whatever sits in the gem, and the setting never goes behind that word. A soul gem takes a **copy of behaviour**. What a soul actually is remains outside the setting's competence. Never write a line that defines it.

**3. Where a game shows an afterlife, it is a machine, not a truth.** Any location the player physically enters and finds the dead in is an **archive**: an installation that stored copies. What walks and speaks there is a copy. Mortals call it the hall of the dead because mortals always do, and that is the unreliable narrator (Part 8) doing its job. This makes a claim about one machine and no claim about death.

The one exception is Part 7, which states that there is a single Maker, that he is not any party inside the setting, and that he cannot be perceived. That is a floor under the world. It is said once, by one character, in one game. It is not a theme to develop.

# Part 1 — Premise `SETTLED`

There is no magic. There is one non-human species with technology no one else can replicate, and a planet full of people who mistake that technology for divinity.

The species is called the **Zenar**.

---

# Part 2 — Background Glossary

For anyone joining the project who does not know the source material.

* **Nirn** the planet. Tamriel is a continent on it; Morrowind and Skyrim are provinces.
* **Oblivion** where the Zenar live. Not "hell", simply not-here.
* **Aedra and Daedra** the two factions of the same species. See Part 3.
* **Heart of Lorkhan** an object buried inside Red Mountain in Vvardenfell. A reactor. See Part 4.
* **Dwemer** an extinct people who built machines. Vanished in a single instant, all of them, thousands of years ago. See Part 5.

Game-specific terms are in the game-specific canon files.

# Part 3 — The Schism `SETTLED`

One species, split by an argument over a construction project.

Nirn already existed. The Zenar undertook its **terraforming**: turning the rivers, salting the seas, making the atmosphere breathable, seeding the first biosphere, correcting the axial tilt until the year held four seasons.

One faction paid for the work out of their own substance: energy, bodies, manufacturing base. The place became habitable. But those who paid were exhausted by it and are now bound to what they reworked. They cannot leave. They spent very nearly everything they had and came close to collapsing back into whatever they had risen out of.

The other faction declined to contribute. They kept their full capability and their freedom of movement. They are stronger for the simple reason that they never spent anything.

**They did not make the world. They renovated it, and it ruined them.** What remains of the first faction is a biological trace, not a share of ownership: an endosymbiont inside every living cell on Nirn, a descendant of an outsider that stopped being an outsider. Mortals do not know it is there.

The mortal terms follow directly from this and are literally accurate:

* **Zenad** "our ancestors." The faction that spent itself.
* **Zenar** "not our ancestors." The faction that did not.

The near-identity of the two words is the clue that they are one species. A rewritten passage from a knowledgeable source should point this out explicitly.

**There is no good and evil here.** There is an old grievance about who paid the bill. Mortals moralised it afterwards, because mortals moralise everything.

`ISLAM CONSISTENCY` Part 0 rule 1 governs this section. Every draft of every line about the Zenad must survive the test: does it credit them with creation, or with greatness? If yes, rewrite.

# Part 4 — Lorkhan and the Heart `SETTLED`

Lorkhan proposed the project: not the making of a world, but the reworking of one that was already there. When it went wrong, or when the cost became clear, the others tore out his power core and dropped it on the planet he had talked them into rebuilding. It came down inside Red Mountain.

The Heart of Lorkhan is a **reactor**. Everything downstream of that follows without strain, and the details are in the Morrowind canon.

---

# Part 5 — The Dwemer `SETTLED`

The Dwemer are not a second technological species. They are the mortals who **found Zenar hardware and reverse-engineered it**. Their machines are derivative work: competent, impressive, and not original.

Kagrenak went into the reactor itself. The entire people vanished in one instant, which is exactly what happens when you make a mistake at that scale.

This resolves the niche collision: only one species invented the technology. Everyone else is doing archaeology. That includes the Dwemer sites in Skyrim, Blackreach and the Aetherium works.

# Part 6 — The Sixteen `SETTLED`

Not gods with domains. Sixteen factions or individuals with incompatible specialisations and long-standing grudges. Gods do not have domains and rivalries; research groups do.

* **Azura** long-range prediction. The Nerevarine prophecy is therefore a *forecast*, not a revelation.
* **Sheogorath** cognition and perception. Locals call the results madness.
* **Hircine** genetic engineering. Werewolves are engineered, not cursed. This covers Skyrim's lycanthropy and vampirism.
* **Mehrunes Dagon** demolition and controlled collapse.
* **Molag Bal** coercion and behavioural control.

**Why no one can replicate it:** the manufacturing base belonged to the faction that spent itself terraforming. It is gone. Nobody can rebuild it, including the Zenar themselves. They are living off inventory. This also explains why they are territorial about it.

**This is the hinge on which the Skyrim plot turns.** An installation with no possible repair, degrading for thousands of years, is the precondition for everything in that game. See *Skyrim Canon* Part 4.

Keep the word **"Prince."** It is what ignorant locals call them, and locals are the ones writing the books. See Part 9.

# Part 7 — The Maker `SETTLED`

Demoting the Zenar from gods to engineers leaves a throne empty. This section fills it, and it is the one place in the setting where the answer is not technological.

**There is one maker of time and matter. It is not the Zenar, not the Zenad, not the Tribunal, and it cannot be perceived.**

This does not violate the no-mysticism rule; it completes it. Everything *inside* the world has a mechanical explanation. The existence of the world is a separate question, outside the system, and the setting answers it once and then stops talking about it.

## The reason it cannot be seen is categorical, not technical

Two earlier drafts were cut, and the reasons are worth keeping so they are not reinvented.

*"An instrument does not measure the hand that forged it."* Cut: false. We build MRI scanners that then measure us.

*"Our eyes are assembled out of the world and cannot see what preceded it."* Cut: it is an argument about **resolution**, and every argument about resolution implies that a better instrument would eventually work.

The correct footing is **containment**, expressed as an image rather than an argument. A drawing cannot see the hand that drew it. A line on a page cannot lift its eyes off the page. Not because the line is too faint, but because "off the page" is not a direction that exists for it. Refining the line does not help. Improving the instrument moves you further into the world, never out of it.

Any rewording must keep that footing.

## Imperceptible does not mean absent

State the asymmetry explicitly wherever this comes up: he is not seen, and he sees. A thing can be nearer to you than your own throat and still not be a thing you can turn and look at.

Without this the passage reads as deism, which is not what the setting is saying.

## Delivery

**Said once, by one character, in one game.** The current delivery is a Vivec monologue in Morrowind's main quest, recorded in *Morrowind Canon*. Skyrim does not restate it. Repeat it and the setting becomes a sermon; it is a floor under the world, not a theme.

# Part 8 — The Unreliable Narrator `SETTLED`

Almost every text in either game was written by someone on Nirn who sincerely believes this is magic and these are gods. **Which means almost every text is already correct and needs no changes at all.**

Rewriting is limited to sources that *know*: Dwemer records, Imperial technical reports, Zenar artefacts themselves, and a small set of named individuals.

The player assembles the truth from the contradiction between what the Temple says and what a Dwemer engineer wrote down.

This turns the project from "rewrite the entire game" into "write twenty books and a dozen notes."

**In Skyrim this principle is not merely useful, it is load-bearing.** Skyrim has roughly sixty thousand voiced lines. Every mortal saying "Divines", "magic", "soul" or "Daedra" out loud is correct by construction, and no audio file needs touching. Any exception to the principle costs an unfixable audio desync.

# Part 9 — The Rename Test `SETTLED`

Rename a word only if the word names the alien thing itself. Keep it if the word is a mortal's interpretation.

| Word | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daedra | **Rename** | Names the species |
| Daedric (material) | **Rename** | Names their manufacturing |
| Prince | Keep | A mortal's word for them |
| Shrine | Keep | A mortal's building |
| Worship | Keep | A mortal's behaviour, accurately described |
| Summon | Keep | What it looks like from outside |
| Soul | Keep | A mortal's word for whatever is in the gem. Load-bearing, see Part 0 rule 2 |
| Dragonborn, dragon blood | Keep | A mortal's description of observed heritable compatibility. See *Skyrim Canon* Part 2 |

Applying this test aggressively is what keeps the change set small.

## One carve-out: DIAL topic IDs `SETTLED`

The test above says what a word *should* become. It does not override the settled topic-ID policy in *Conversion Architecture* Part 5.

A DIAL record has no separate display field, so its ID is what the player reads in the topic list. Renaming it would break every mod that references that topic, present or future. **Topic IDs are therefore never touched, in either game**, and the topic list will read `Daedra`.

This is not a defeat. Under Part 8 the topic list is the player character's own list of subjects, phrased in the vernacular they learned it in. Locals say Daedra. The player learned the word from locals. It is correct.

Where the naming table below names a word that is also a topic ID, the replacement applies to prose occurrences only.

## Why mortals say "Daedra" `SETTLED`

**This solves the voiced-audio problem completely. No audio file needs replacing.**

The etymology is already in the source material: in old Aldmeris, **"Daedra" means "not our ancestors."** It was never a name. It is a category, coined by people sorting the world into ours and not-ours. The Zenar call themselves Zenar. Mortals never asked.

Therefore:

* Every voiced line in which a mortal says "Daedra" is **correct by construction**.
* Dremora combat barks survive untouched. A Zenar soldier throwing a local's own word back at him is exactly in character.
* No text needs a disclaimer. The two vocabularies simply coexist, which is what happens in any real contact situation.

**And the Temple kept the word on purpose.** Call a thing kin and you owe it something. Call it not-kin and you may do as you please. The word does political work, which is why it survived.

This is the single highest-leverage decision in the project: it converts an unsolvable problem into a characterisation opportunity.

## The three senses of "Daedric" `SETTLED`

`Daedric` carries four unrelated meanings in the original. Measured against the
masters on 2026-08-28, three of them are live in the text and each takes a
different word:

| Sense | Word | Where it fires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Made by them | **Zenaric** | equipment, materials, artefacts, their script |
| Of their cult | **Zetic** | ruins, shrines, sites, cults, worship, standing stones, statues |
| The beings themselves | **Zenar** | servants, lords, princes, forces, messengers, minions, summonings |

The third sense coins nothing: the beings are Zenar, so a Zenar servant is a
servant who is one of them, exactly as in the settled `Daedra Lord` to
`Zenar Lord` row above. Without it, `Daedric servants` becomes
`Zenaric servants` — servants *manufactured* by them — which is a different
claim about the world and a false one.

Counted over the merged masters: Zenaric 128, Zetic 118, Zenar 48. The cult
sense is the one people underestimate; `Daedric ruin` alone fires 80 times.

## The two adjectives are the point

`Daedric` carries four unrelated meanings in the original, and one substitution rule for all four produces nonsense. Splitting it into **two** adjectives solves this lexically:

* **Zenaric** made by the Zenar. Equipment, materials, artefacts, their script.
* **Zetic** pertaining to the Zenar *cult*. Shrines, ruins, worshippers, rites.

A Zetic Shrine is a building **mortals** built. There is no such thing as Zenar architecture on Nirn. Getting this wrong populates the map with alien temples that do not exist in your fiction.

Because `Daedric` to `Zenaric` is length-preserving, **every equipment name is automatically safe** from truncation.

> **Safe is not the same as reachable.** Work Order 0 measured that armour, weapon, clothing and creature names cannot be written from Lua in OpenMW 0.51, from any context. This table states what the words *should* become; *Conversion Architecture* Part 12 states which of them can currently be delivered. Do not read a row here as a solved problem.

## Substring warning `SETTLED, MEASURED`

**The string "daedra" contains the string "aedra".** Work Order 1's first pass matched substrings, and almost every reported hit for `aedra` was a false one. The re-run of 2026-08-28 applied a left word boundary and measured what is really there:

| Field | First pass claimed | Real occurrences | Real records |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| INFO text | 338 | **22** | 22 |
| BOOK text | 84 | **38** | 13 |
| BOOK name | 5 | **2** | 2 |
| DIAL id | 13 | **0** | 0 |
| INGREDIENT name | 3 | **0** | 0 |

Two consequences, both mandatory in any rules table, in either game:

* `aedra` must carry a **left word boundary**. Without it the transform turns every "Daedra" into "DZenad", mechanically and identically across the whole game.
* Rule order must be **fixed and versioned**: `daedra` before `aedra`, always.

Consequence for scope: `Aedra` to `Zenad` touches **35 records game-wide, 62 occurrences**. It is a cosmetic decision, not a structural one.

A third consequence, learned the same day and about the tooling rather than the words: the boundary is worth nothing if the matcher treats the pattern as a regular expression. Lua's `string.gsub` does, and offers no plain-match flag; only `string.find` has one. Match literally, in both games.

## Free of charge, rename nothing

These contain no "daedra" root and need zero records touched:

Dremora, Scamp, Clannfear, Golden Saint, Winged Twilight, Ogrim, Hunger, Atronach (Flame/Frost/Storm).

They already read as alien species names.

The **Daedric alphabet** on textures requires no work whatsoever. It already reads as an alien writing system.

> **This table is a starting point, not an inventory.** Generate the authoritative list mechanically before writing rules. Assume it contains forms not listed here.

# Part 11 — Technology Requires an Interface `SETTLED`

Zenar technology does not operate on intention. It requires an interface to address it.

* Natives have theirs integrated: congenital, inherited, or implanted young.
* Outlanders and latecomers have nothing and must wear a device.
* **The device is the gate.** Whoever holds one has access; whoever does not, does not.

This single principle carries across both games and produces the same mechanic twice:

| Game | Implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Morrowind | The player wears an issued device. Without it, spellcasting is gated. See *Morrowind Canon* Part 8 |
| Skyrim | Dragon priest masks are worn interface devices. The Dragon Cult is not a religion, it is administrators who reached the console. See *Skyrim Canon* Part 3 |

One mechanic, two games, no strain. When designing anything new in either game, check whether this principle already answers it before inventing a second system.
