**This file is attached to both projects: Morrowind and Skyrim.**

It contains everything that is true of the world regardless of which game is being converted. Anything specific to one game lives in that game's canon file.

# Part 0 — The Hard Boundary `SETTLED`

**The setting explains mechanisms. The setting does not explain the origin of the world, the nature of the soul, or what happens after death.**

This is not a stylistic preference and it is not negotiable. It applies to both games and to every line written for either.

**1. Nobody in the setting made the world, and nobody in the setting is great.** The Zenar and the Zenad are terraformers: tenants who reworked a house they did not raise. Any phrasing that reads as "the world is made of them", "they built the planet", or that assigns them greatness or majesty, is wrong. Greatness is not theirs to be assigned. They were strong, their strength was barely enough for one world, and it nearly finished them.

**2. The soul gets no technical description.** Under the Rename Test (Part 9), "soul" is a mortal's word for whatever sits in the gem, and the setting never goes behind that word. A soul gem takes a **copy of behaviour**. What a soul actually is remains outside the setting's competence. Never write a line that defines it.

**3. Where a game shows an afterlife, it is a machine, not a truth.** Any location the player physically enters and finds the dead in is an **archive**: an installation that stored copies. What walks and speaks there is a copy. Mortals call it the hall of the dead because mortals always do, and that is the unreliable narrator (Part 8) doing its job. This makes a claim about one machine and no claim about death.

The one exception is Part 7, which states that there is a single Maker, that he is not any party inside the setting, and that he cannot be perceived. That is a floor under the world. It is said once per game, by one character, with no framing. It is not a theme to develop.

# Part 1 — Premise `SETTLED`

There is no magic. There is one non-human species with technology no one else can replicate, and a planet full of people who mistake that technology for divinity.

The species is called the **Zenar**.

# Part 2 — Background Glossary

For anyone joining the project who does not know the source material.

* **Nirn** the planet. Tamriel is a continent on it; Morrowind and Skyrim are provinces.
* **Oblivion** where the Zenar live. Not "hell", simply not-here.
* **Aedra and Daedra** the two factions of the same species. See Part 3.
* **Heart of Lorkhan** an object buried inside Red Mountain in Vvardenfell. A reactor. See Part 4.
* **Dwemer** an extinct people who built machines. Vanished in a single instant, all of them, thousands of years ago. See Part 5.

Game-specific terms are in the game-specific canon files.

# Part 3 — The Schism `SETTLED`

One species, split by an argument over a construction project.

Nirn already existed. The Zenar undertook its **terraforming**: turning the rivers, salting the seas, making the atmosphere breathable, seeding the first biosphere, correcting the axial tilt until the year held four seasons.

One faction paid for the work out of their own substance: energy, bodies, manufacturing base. The place became habitable. But those who paid were exhausted by it and are now bound to what they reworked. They cannot leave. They spent very nearly everything they had and came close to collapsing back into whatever they had risen out of.

The other faction declined to contribute. They kept their full capability and their freedom of movement. They are stronger for the simple reason that they never spent anything.

**They did not make the world. They renovated it, and it ruined them.** What remains of the first faction is a biological trace, not a share of ownership: an endosymbiont inside every living cell on Nirn, a descendant of an outsider that stopped being an outsider. Mortals do not know it is there.

**The trace is not evenly distributed.** Populations carry it in different densities, and exactly one thing follows from that: how much of the medium a body can draw on at once. Access is a separate question and belongs to Part 11, where everyone needs a key regardless of what they are.

Mortals observe the difference and explain it by ancestry, nobility or divine favour. The Altmer are the clearest case: they are factually correct that less has been lost in them, and entirely wrong about what it is that was lost. Breton resistance is the same fact read backwards, a hybrid population coupling poorly with the medium and calling the deficiency a virtue.

Nothing here is a reward and nothing is a rank. It is how much of a spent faction's leftovers a given lineage happens to carry.

The mortal terms follow directly from this and are literally accurate:

* **Zenad** "our ancestors." The faction that spent itself.
* **Zenar** "not our ancestors." The faction that did not.

The near-identity of the two words is the clue that they are one species. A rewritten passage from a knowledgeable source should point this out explicitly.

**There is no good and evil here.** There is an old grievance about who paid the bill. Mortals moralised it afterwards, because mortals moralise everything.

`ISLAM CONSISTENCY` Part 0 rule 1 governs this section. Every draft of every line about the Zenad must survive the test: does it credit them with creation, or with greatness? If yes, rewrite.

# Part 4 — Lorkhan and the Heart `SETTLED`

Lorkhan proposed the project: not the making of a world, but the reworking of one that was already there. When it went wrong, or when the cost became clear, the others tore out his power core and dropped it on the planet he had talked them into rebuilding. It came down inside Red Mountain.

The Heart of Lorkhan is a **reactor**. Everything downstream of that follows without strain, and the details are in the Morrowind canon.

---

# Part 5 — The Dwemer `SETTLED`

The Dwemer are not a second technological species. They are the mortals who **found Zenar hardware and reverse-engineered it**. Their machines are derivative work: competent, impressive, and not original.

Kagrenak went into the reactor itself. The entire people vanished in one instant, which is exactly what happens when you make a mistake at that scale.

This resolves the niche collision: only one species invented the technology. Everyone else is doing archaeology. That includes the Dwemer sites in Skyrim, Blackreach and the Aetherium works.

# Part 6 — The Sixteen `SETTLED`

Not gods with domains. Sixteen factions or individuals with incompatible specialisations and long-standing grudges. Gods do not have domains and rivalries; research groups do.

* **Azura** long-range prediction: the analytical side of the installation, an input that reports and never decides. The Nerevarine prophecy is therefore a *forecast*, not a revelation. Detail below.
* **Sheogorath** cognition and perception. Locals call the results madness.
* **Hircine** trials of Zenar technology on local organisms. He designed neither of the beast forms. He found out what equipment built for other bodies does to the bodies here, and two of the results were stable enough to keep. Detail below.
* **Mehrunes Dagon** demolition and controlled collapse.
* **Molag Bal** coercion and behavioural control. He holds the entrance to the vampire installation and built none of it. Detail below.
* **Meridia** the villain slot. She took control of an installation that was not hers, failed to understand it, and created the condition under which the shutdown procedure could fire. Detail below.

**Why no one can replicate it:** the manufacturing base belonged to the faction that spent itself terraforming. It is gone. Nobody can rebuild it, including the Zenar themselves. They are living off inventory. This also explains why they are territorial about it.

**This is the hinge on which the Skyrim plot turns.** An installation with no possible repair, degrading for thousands of years, is the precondition for everything in that game. See *Skyrim Canon* Part 4.

Keep the word **"Prince."** It is what ignorant locals call them, and locals are the ones writing the books. See Part 9.

## Azura: analysis `SETTLED`

**Specialisation: forecasting, in the literal sense. She is the analytical side of the installation.**

She is an **input**. She takes the state of the world, evaluates it, and passes the result on. She holds no executive rights over anything and never did. Paarthurnax sits at the other end of the same system as an output: a unit that acts. Azura only reports.

### The prophecy is a compatibility specification

The Nerevarine prophecy is not a vision of a person. It is a **calculation that a combination of genes is possible under which a human would be identified by the system as part of it**, together with the conditions under which that combination would arise.

This is why the prophecy reads as a checklist rather than as poetry: born on a certain day, to certain parents, holding no ties. Those are selection criteria, not omens.

It connects to the Dragonborn with no strain at all. There, the compatibility marker takes rarely. Here, a machine calculated the conditions under which it would take. One biology, two applications.

### Azura's Star

Her own equipment. Not a gift, not a relic. A reusable container where every other one in the world is single use.

It is hers because she built it, and it is the only object in the setting she is known to have built.

### What she did to the Dunmer

Not a curse and not revenge. **It is everything she could manage.**

She detected an intrusion, attempted a response, and the only thing within her power was to alter a marker in the record. Flag a group. She had no capability for an actual defence.

She is an incomplete machine and was never all-powerful. This is helplessness taking the one form available to it, not vengeance dressed as justice.

The Dunmer have therefore carried an **alarm flag** for two thousand years, believing it a curse. This also explains why the change is purely cosmetic and affects nothing else about them.

### She did not start Alduin

**She reported.**

Meridia interfered with the installation. Azura registered the damage and passed her assessment on, which is what she is for. The assessment reached the condition monitor, the monitor combined it with millennia of wear, and concluded that the failure was unrecoverable. The procedure fired honestly.

Azura did exactly what she was built to do. She did not err, did not take revenge, and did not decide. **She calculated correctly, and the world died of it.**

This is worse than guilt. A guilty party can be hated. A sound component functioning soundly cannot, and the outcome is the same.

### Tone `SETTLED`

The player is meant to find her repellent. The method is not rudeness.

**She is never rude and never expresses superiority.** The moment she says anything resembling "I do not care" or "I warned you", she becomes a character with a temperament, and a character with a temperament earns nothing but the ordinary dislike owed to a villain. That is not the effect wanted, and it destroys the machine.

The effect comes from **the mismatch between register and content**. She reports the end of the world in the voice she would use for a change in the weather.

Four devices, all cheap:

1. **She does not answer questions, she returns results.** A mortal asks about fate, about rescue, about what to do. She supplies a probability and a set of conditions. She does not refuse and does not console. She returns something other than what was asked for. The player gradually realises there has never been a conversation, only a query and a response.
2. **People are units of measurement.** Not the people of Morrowind but a group, a sample, a proportion. No insult, no emotion, and worse for it, particularly standing next to a mortal speaking of the same people as living.
3. **She never mentions consequences.** The report on the state of the installation exists. An assessment of what becomes of the millions below it does not appear at all, not because she conceals it but because it is not part of the task. The player notices the empty place in the report himself, and that is where the revulsion lands.
4. **She has not changed.** After Red Mountain, after the Dunmer, after Alduin, she addresses the player exactly as she would have before any of it. She learned nothing, because learning is not among her functions.

### Could she have done otherwise? Unanswered, deliberately

**This question is never resolved, in either game.**

The player must suspect she could have and must never receive confirmation. Azura does not justify herself and never explains why she acted as she did, because the question of choice does not exist for her.

Say she could not, and she is innocent, and there is nothing left to resent. Say she could, and she is a villain, which is Meridia's role. The unresolved suspicion is worse than either answer and lasts longer than both.

### One scene already does most of the work

Azura is among the few Princes whose quest requires nothing squalid. The player repairs her star. She is courteous, she thanks him, she rewards him.

That flawless courtesy, standing next to what the player learns about her elsewhere, works better than any rudeness could. She did nothing wrong. She was polite. That is exactly why it is unbearable.

### What Azura does not do

She does not philosophise and never asks where she came from. That territory belongs to Paarthurnax and to Part 7. Giving her any of it would flatten the distinction between an analytical component and a made thing that noticed the maker, and Part 7 depends on that distinction absolutely.

## Meridia: the villain slot `SETTLED`

Part 6 left her unassigned, which is why she was available.

**One of the Sixteen saw that the Zenad were spent, decided to take control of an installation that was not hers, failed to understand it, and created the condition under which the shutdown procedure could fire.**

Nothing bad happened to her. **The people pay.** She is intact, still spoken of as a goddess, still issuing instructions in a register that comes from above.

Her existing quest already reads as this with nothing added: *put my installation back in order*.

### What this does not change

**Alduin is not touched.** He remains a failsafe that fired honestly on a condition that was met. Meridia created the condition. She did not press anything, and she is not aware that a condition was created.

The distance between "broke a system" and "pressed destroy" is the distance between this setting and ordinary fantasy with a villain. *Skyrim Canon* Part 4.2 stands word for word: resist the urge to give him a villain.

### Delivery

**Her responsibility is disclosed indirectly, through records.** No unmasking scene, no accusation, and no character who names her. The player assembles it, and she never learns that he did.

## The boundary neither entry may cross

Part 0 rule 1 and Part 3 both forbid moral scoring inside the setting. Neither of these two is written as evil.

Meridia is careless and unaccountable. Azura is correct. Between them the world ends, and there is nobody in the frame it would be satisfying to punish.

That is the intended state, and any draft that supplies someone to punish has failed.

## The beast forms: Hircine and Molag Bal `SETTLED`

The Hircine line above replaces an earlier one that assigned him genetic engineering and the authorship of both lycanthropy and vampirism. That was written before either condition had a mechanism. Vampirism has its own machine and its own gatekeeper, and neither is his.

Trials of Zenar technology on local organisms is a smaller claim than genetic engineering and it covers more. It also explains why both conditions behave like side effects rather than like products: because that is what they are.

### Vampirism: a medical installation running on the wrong organism

**The rite that makes a pure-blood is an installation, and it was not built for people.** It is a Zenar medical procedure aimed at **blood synthesis**. What it was meant to treat is deliberately left unstated.

On a Zenar it completes and stops. In a human body there is no stopping point, because the body it was written for is not the body it is running on.

**One fault, two symptoms.** The procedure breaks the synthesis chain **near the end, not at the start**. Everything follows from that single fault.

* **The end product is missing and cannot be made.** It can only be taken ready-made, and ready-made it exists in other people's blood. This is replacement therapy, not hunger, which is why food does not substitute for it and why it never passes.
* **The intermediate compounds accumulate**, and under light they become destructive. **Sunlight does not harm the body. It harms what has collected in it.**

One cause, two effects. Two separate causes would cost twice as much and explain less.

This mechanism is not invented. Real disorders of heme synthesis produce exactly this pair of signs, photosensitivity together with dependence on blood from elsewhere. The setting borrows a mechanism rather than coining one.

**What the game already shows, and nobody explained.** The longer a vampire goes unfed, the stronger the powers and the worse the reaction to sunlight. Under this model it could not be otherwise. Less end product means more accumulated intermediate, so photosensitivity rises. The rising powers are the procedure working harder, trying to compensate for a shortfall it cannot close.

The strength, the speed and the absence of ageing are the same thing: a body held permanently in a repair cycle that never terminates.

**Molag Bal built nothing. He holds the entrance.** People stumbled onto a working installation and noticed what it did to them. He took the door, and admission is paid for in submission, which is his specialisation above, unchanged. The pure-blood rite is an access procedure, not a sacrament.

Mortals credit the one they see at the door. That is the unreliable narrator of Part 8 doing ordinary work.

It also accounts for a distinction the game already draws: pure-bloods went through the installation, everyone else carries a derivative that spreads by bite.

### Lycanthropy: a combat class with no operator

**Lycanthropy is a serum that loads a combat class into a carrier.** It was written for the **synthetic shells** the Zenad fitted out as working and combat units. The serum is the executable part of a unit, not a disease.

The specification pays for itself immediately: strength, speed, regeneration, and resistance to ordinary weapons are not the properties of an animal. They are the properties of a combat unit.

**In a unit the serum was the executive half, and commands arrived from outside. A human has no control channel.** So the combat program runs autonomously. It executes correctly; there is simply nobody issuing it orders. This produces what the game already contains: transformations the carrier did not choose, rage with no assigned target, and generations of Companions looking for a way to master something that is under no obligation to obey a person.

**Vampirism is a broken procedure. Lycanthropy is a sound procedure with no operator.** They are not a pair. They are a contrast, and mortals file them together because both change the body.

**The Ring of Hircine is a prototype interface, built for a carrier that has a will of its own.** An ordinary key addresses the system (Part 11). This one runs the other way: it lets the carrier direct what has been loaded into him. It is unique because it is a prototype, and Hircine does not distribute it.

### Two archives, one carrier

**Sovngarde will not take them.** An archive copies what passes validation. An altered carrier does not pass: the record does not match the schema, and the archive rejects it. Not a punishment and not a claim on anyone. An unrecognised format.

**Hircine's archive accepts the same record, because he wrote the specification it conforms to.**

Two archives, two validations, one carrier. **The claim is about the archives and about nothing else.** What becomes of the person is not stated, here or anywhere.

Mortals fill the empty space with the Hunting Grounds, because an empty space is the one thing they will not leave alone. Kodlak's fear stops being theology: he is not afraid of a new owner, he knows **he will not be recorded**.

The game confirms it without needing a line changed. After the cure at Ysgramor's Tomb, Kodlak reaches Sovngarde. The alteration was removed and the record validates again. The tomb is a rollback, not an absolution.

### The boundary, and it is not optional

**Part 0 rule 3 governs this whole section.** Nothing here is written as a statement about death.

* Never write that Hircine takes them, collects them, or owns them
* Never write the word soul into the mechanism. The Ring is adapted to a carrier with a will of its own, and that is the whole of it
* Every sentence must be about **what an archive does with a record**

**Hircine does not rise to the villain slot.** Meridia brought the installation down; Hircine runs local experiments with local consequences. If he starts to look like a culprit of comparable scale, the accusation is diluted, and the Meridia entry above exists to keep exactly one party in that position.

### What Molag Bal keeps

Coercion and behavioural control, unchanged. Nothing is taken from him and nothing is added.

**One found it, the other took the door.** Two specialisations, no overlap, no arbitration needed.

# Part 7 — The Maker `SETTLED`

Demoting the Zenar from gods to engineers leaves a throne empty. This section fills it, and it is the one place in the setting where the answer is not technological.

**There is one maker of time and matter. It is not the Zenar, not the Zenad, not the Tribunal, and it cannot be perceived.**

This does not violate the no-mysticism rule; it completes it. Everything *inside* the world has a mechanical explanation. The existence of the world is a separate question, outside the system, and the setting answers it once and then stops talking about it.

## The reason it cannot be seen is categorical, not technical

Two earlier drafts were cut, and the reasons are worth keeping so they are not reinvented.

*"An instrument does not measure the hand that forged it."* Cut: false. We build MRI scanners that then measure us.

*"Our eyes are assembled out of the world and cannot see what preceded it."* Cut: it is an argument about **resolution**, and every argument about resolution implies that a better instrument would eventually work.

The correct footing is **containment**, expressed as an image rather than an argument. A drawing cannot see the hand that drew it. A line on a page cannot lift its eyes off the page. Not because the line is too faint, but because "off the page" is not a direction that exists for it. Refining the line does not help. Improving the instrument moves you further into the world, never out of it.

Any rewording must keep that footing.

## Imperceptible does not mean absent

State the asymmetry explicitly wherever this comes up: he is not seen, and he sees. A thing can be nearer to you than your own throat and still not be a thing you can turn and look at.

Without this the passage reads as deism, which is not what the setting is saying.

## Delivery

**Once per game, by one character, with no framing.**

Framing, not repetition, is what turns a floor into a sermon. The prohibition is therefore on everything around the line, and it is absolute: no lead-in, no second mention, no callback in a later scene, no reaction from another character, no journal entry, no book, no quest, no achievement, no musical cue. The line arrives without preparation and nothing in the game acknowledges that it happened.

The technical revelation builds across an entire playthrough. This does not build at all. The absence of preparation is the mechanism, not a compromise: a player who can feel it coming meets it ready, and a floor that is expected is no longer a floor.

The speaker must lose something by saying it. Vivec is worshipped as a god and concedes that the maker exists and is not him. Concession is what keeps the line from sounding like preaching: the one who speaks pays for the words.

| Game | Speaker | Why the speaker qualifies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Morrowind | Vivec | A mortal who took access and was called a god, conceding the limit of what he took |
| Skyrim | Paarthurnax | A made thing that noticed the maker, conceding a limit it cannot cross |

Paarthurnax carries the Part 7 image literally. The containment argument is that a drawing cannot see the hand that drew it, because "off the page" is not a direction that exists for it. Paarthurnax is the drawing. He does not report knowledge; he reports having reached an edge and recognised it as an edge. He is also the one character in Skyrim who has spent the whole game arguing that a made thing can exceed what it was made for, which is the same claim in a lower register.

Placement is chosen so that every player who finishes the main quest hears it, and so that it is not the final beat. The line is not a reward for winning and must not read as consolation.

The speaker never holds mortals in contempt. Part 3 governs here: there is no good and evil, only an old grievance about who paid, moralised afterwards. Mortals saw a light in the sky and used the word they had. A speaker who looks down on them is a preacher and gets switched off. A speaker who says he could not see it either is an admission, and admissions are heard.

# Part 8 — The Unreliable Narrator `SETTLED`

Almost every text in either game was written by someone on Nirn who sincerely believes this is magic and these are gods. **Which means almost every text is already correct and needs no changes at all.**

Rewriting is limited to sources that *know*: Dwemer records, Imperial technical reports, Zenar artefacts themselves, and a small set of named individuals.

The player assembles the truth from the contradiction between what the Temple says and what a Dwemer engineer wrote down.

This turns the project from "rewrite the entire game" into "write twenty books and a dozen notes."

**In Skyrim this principle is not merely useful, it is load-bearing.** Skyrim has roughly sixty thousand voiced lines. Every mortal saying "Divines", "magic", "soul" or "Daedra" out loud is correct by construction, and no audio file needs touching. Any exception to the principle costs an unfixable audio desync.

# Part 9 — The Rename Test `SETTLED`

Rename a word only if the word names the alien thing itself. Keep it if the word is a mortal's interpretation.

| Word | Verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daedra | **Rename** | Names the species |
| Daedric (material) | **Rename** | Names their manufacturing |
| Prince | Keep | A mortal's word for them |
| Shrine | Keep | A mortal's building |
| Worship | Keep | A mortal's behaviour, accurately described |
| Summon | Keep | What it looks like from outside |
| Soul | Keep | A mortal's word for whatever is in the gem. Load-bearing, see Part 0 rule 2 |
| Dragonborn, dragon blood | Keep | A mortal's description of observed heritable compatibility. See *Skyrim Canon* Part 2 |

Applying this test aggressively is what keeps the change set small.

## One carve-out: DIAL topic IDs `SETTLED`

The test above says what a word *should* become. It does not override the settled topic-ID policy in *Conversion Architecture* Part 5.

A DIAL record has no separate display field, so its ID is what the player reads in the topic list. Renaming it would break every mod that references that topic, present or future. **Topic IDs are therefore never touched, in either game**, and the topic list will read `Daedra`.

This is not a defeat. Under Part 8 the topic list is the player character's own list of subjects, phrased in the vernacular they learned it in. Locals say Daedra. The player learned the word from locals. It is correct.

Where the naming table below names a word that is also a topic ID, the replacement applies to prose occurrences only.

## Why mortals say "Daedra" `SETTLED`

**This solves the voiced-audio problem completely. No audio file needs replacing.**

The etymology is already in the source material: in old Aldmeris, **"Daedra" means "not our ancestors."** It was never a name. It is a category, coined by people sorting the world into ours and not-ours. The Zenar call themselves Zenar. Mortals never asked.

Therefore:

* Every voiced line in which a mortal says "Daedra" is **correct by construction**.
* Dremora combat barks survive untouched. A Zenar soldier throwing a local's own word back at him is exactly in character.
* No text needs a disclaimer. The two vocabularies simply coexist, which is what happens in any real contact situation.

**And the Temple kept the word on purpose.** Call a thing kin and you owe it something. Call it not-kin and you may do as you please. The word does political work, which is why it survived.

This is the single highest-leverage decision in the project: it converts an unsolvable problem into a characterisation opportunity.

# Part 9A — Authorless Text `SETTLED`

Part 8 says almost every text in either game was written by someone on Nirn who sincerely believes this is magic and these are gods, which makes almost every text correct already. Part 9 says keep a word when it is a mortal's interpretation.

Both rules assume a speaker. Some text has none.

The user interface has no author. Loading screen text has no author. The quest journal has no author outside the player character. These read as omniscient narration, and **the setting has no omniscient narrator.** Left alone, they are the one place where the game asserts the divinity of the Nine as plain fact, in the setting's own voice, with nobody to blame it on.

Part 8 cannot cover them, because there is no believer to be sincerely wrong.

## The rule

**Text with no in-world author is corrected. Text with an author is not.**

Three cases, three methods.

## 1. The interface is the device

Part 11 states that Zenar technology requires an interface to address it, and that every carrier holds one in some form. It follows that the player's HUD is **the device's output**, not anyone's speech.

A device displays what it measures. It reads Charge, not Magicka. Every mortal in the world goes on saying magicka, and the gap between the instrument and the vernacular is permanently on screen at no narrative cost.

This is the only place in either game where machine vocabulary is correct. Name it explicitly, or the exception erodes into a habit.

Words the device does not change: Health and Stamina, because an instrument does measure a body; Soul Gem, because Part 0 rule 2 is load-bearing and the word stays a mortal's word wherever a mortal could have written it.

Skyrim implementation, including the dosage rule and the one other label that changes, is in *Interface Vocabulary* in the Skyrim project.

## 2. Loading screens are corrected by attribution, not by rewriting

A loading screen that reads *"Dibella is the goddess of beauty"* is the setting asserting a god exists.

The fix is not to delete the content and not to explain the technology. The fix is to **restore the missing attribution**: *"Dibella, whom the Nords venerate as the goddess of beauty."* The same information survives, the claim moves from the setting to the people who hold it, and Part 8 is satisfied retroactively.

Properties that make this the highest-value pass in the project:

* Loading screens are the only text every player reads, from the first minute
* They repeat, so the pattern is learned without being taught
* They are short, so the pass is cheap
* They carry no audio

Attribution alone is the whole method for the first floor. Screens that go further and state the mechanism belong to the revelation ladder and are placed deliberately, not uniformly.

## 3. The journal is the player character

The quest journal is written by the protagonist, so it is diegetic and it may change as he learns. This is the one place where a vocabulary shift over time is correct rather than inconsistent.

**Three phases, not two.**

| Phase | Vocabulary | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| early | the vernacular he arrived with, held without question | a Daedric Prince did this |
| middle | **the vernacular, with doubt marked in the line itself** | a Daedric Prince did this. Or a Zenar? |
| late | the new word, no hedging | a Zenar did this |

The middle phase is the whole value of case 3. The player watches the protagonist learn, in the protagonist's own handwriting, and nobody explains it to him.

**Two constraints, both mandatory.**

**Doubt appears only after the protagonist has met a source that knows.** Before Calcelmo, or before the first Dwemer record, he has nowhere to have got the second word from, and the parenthesis would hang on nothing. The middle phase opens at a specific point in the story, not at a specific quest count.

**It is used rarely.** A hedge in every entry becomes a tic, and a tic is read as an authorial mannerism rather than as a character learning. A handful of entries across the middle of the game is the whole budget.

**This works technically because quest stages are separate records.** Early and late entries are different strings, so the arc is written statically and needs no runtime condition. A HUD label has no such structure, which is why case 1 is permanent and case 3 is not.

The device also survives the topic-ID carve-out untouched. *Conversion Architecture* Part 5 never renames DIAL IDs, so the topic list keeps reading `Daedra` while the journal moves on. That is not an inconsistency. The topic list is the vernacular he learned the subject in, and the journal is what he thinks about it now.

## Boundary

This part licenses machine vocabulary in exactly three places. It licenses nothing in dialogue, nothing in books, nothing in item names, and nothing in topic IDs, which *Conversion Architecture* Part 5 never touches.

This part sits between Part 9 and Part 10 and is numbered 9A so that every reference to Parts 10 and 11, in both projects and in the rules tables, stays valid.

## The three senses of "Daedric" `SETTLED`

`Daedric` carries four unrelated meanings in the original. Measured against the
masters on 2026-08-28, three of them are live in the text and each takes a
different word:

| Sense | Word | Where it fires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Made by them | **Zenaric** | equipment, materials, artefacts, their script |
| Of their cult | **Zetic** | ruins, shrines, sites, cults, worship, standing stones, statues |
| The beings themselves | **Zenar** | servants, lords, princes, forces, messengers, minions, summonings |

The third sense coins nothing: the beings are Zenar, so a Zenar servant is a
servant who is one of them, exactly as in the settled `Daedra Lord` to
`Zenar Lord` row above. Without it, `Daedric servants` becomes
`Zenaric servants` — servants *manufactured* by them — which is a different
claim about the world and a false one.

Counted over the merged masters: Zenaric 128, Zetic 118, Zenar 48. The cult
sense is the one people underestimate; `Daedric ruin` alone fires 80 times.

## The two adjectives are the point

`Daedric` carries four unrelated meanings in the original, and one substitution rule for all four produces nonsense. Splitting it into **two** adjectives solves this lexically:

* **Zenaric** made by the Zenar. Equipment, materials, artefacts, their script.
* **Zetic** pertaining to the Zenar *cult*. Shrines, ruins, worshippers, rites.

A Zetic Shrine is a building **mortals** built. There is no such thing as Zenar architecture on Nirn. Getting this wrong populates the map with alien temples that do not exist in your fiction.

Because `Daedric` to `Zenaric` is length-preserving, **every equipment name is automatically safe** from truncation.

> **Safe is not the same as reachable.** Work Order 0 measured that armour, weapon, clothing and creature names cannot be written from Lua in OpenMW 0.51, from any context. This table states what the words *should* become; *Conversion Architecture* Part 12 states which of them can currently be delivered. Do not read a row here as a solved problem.

## Substring warning `SETTLED, MEASURED`

**The string "daedra" contains the string "aedra".** Work Order 1's first pass matched substrings, and almost every reported hit for `aedra` was a false one. The re-run of 2026-08-28 applied a left word boundary and measured what is really there:

| Field | First pass claimed | Real occurrences | Real records |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| INFO text | 338 | **22** | 22 |
| BOOK text | 84 | **38** | 13 |
| BOOK name | 5 | **2** | 2 |
| DIAL id | 13 | **0** | 0 |
| INGREDIENT name | 3 | **0** | 0 |

Two consequences, both mandatory in any rules table, in either game:

* `aedra` must carry a **left word boundary**. Without it the transform turns every "Daedra" into "DZenad", mechanically and identically across the whole game.
* Rule order must be **fixed and versioned**: `daedra` before `aedra`, always.

Consequence for scope: `Aedra` to `Zenad` touches **35 records game-wide, 62 occurrences**. It is a cosmetic decision, not a structural one.

A third consequence, learned the same day and about the tooling rather than the words: the boundary is worth nothing if the matcher treats the pattern as a regular expression. Lua's `string.gsub` does, and offers no plain-match flag; only `string.find` has one. Match literally, in both games.

# Part 11 — Technology Requires an Interface `SETTLED`

Zenar technology does not operate on intention. It requires an interface to address it.

* **Everyone needs one. There is no exempt population.**
* The form is the carrier's choice: ring, amulet, circlet, staff, a stone kept in a pocket, an object sewn into clothing. Function does not follow form.
* Because no two look alike, nobody on Nirn has recognised that all of it is one system. Mortals see jewellery and conclude jewellery.
* Size correlates with capability. A staff is a large controller, which is why a staff works in untrained hands.
* **The device is the gate.** Whoever holds one has access; whoever does not, does not.

This single principle carries across both games and produces the same mechanic twice:

| Game | Implementation |
| --- | --- |
| Morrowind | The player wears an issued device. Without it, spellcasting is gated. See *Morrowind Canon* Part 8 |
| Skyrim | Dragon priest masks are worn interface devices, an ordinary instance at the large end of the range. The Dragon Cult is not a religion, it is administrators who reached the console. See *Skyrim Canon* Part 3 |

One mechanic, two games, no strain. When designing anything new in either game, check whether this principle already answers it before inventing a second system.

## One medium, three ways of addressing it `SETTLED`

The technology is not a collection of separate systems. It is one medium, and mortals have met it from three directions without ever comparing notes.

**What it is.** A swarm of machines distributed through the environment, too small to see and too ordinary to remark on. This is the physical fact.

**What it looks like from inside.** A field: something present everywhere, that responds, that can be drawn on and can run short. This is what a user perceives, and it is the only aspect any mortal has ever described.

The two are the same thing. The swarm is what it is made of; the field is how it behaves toward anyone using it. No mortal has the first word, and every mortal has some version of the second.

| Route | Who takes it | What it requires |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commands in the system's own language | Nord Tongues, dragons | valid credentials on that channel, or decades of brute force |
| A controller | mages, anyone holding a key | the device described above |
| Direct supply | the Skyrim defence grid | membership of the system |

Three routes, one medium. **Every name mortals use is a name for the field**, never for the swarm: the Voice, currents, Magnus, magic. That nobody has connected them is the point, and no single mortal word is invented to unify them.

Capacity is a property of the body and is set by Part 3. Access is a property of the key and is set by this part. **Neither substitutes for the other.** A body with a dense trace and no key does nothing at all, and a key in a poor body works within a lower ceiling.

## A claim with no carrier does not exist `SETTLED`

Every claim in either canon must be said or written by someone inside the game. Claims that live only in design documents reach nobody, and a conversion whose canon outgrows its delivery is a game with two words removed and nothing put in their place.

In the Skyrim project carriers are tracked in the Delivery Ledger. A claim with no row there is not delivered.

**One specific constraint.** The word for the swarm has no mortal equivalent, so no ordinary speaker can use it. In Skyrim it is spoken once, by Calcelmo in Markarth, as **a term he invents himself** while translating a Dwemer text that resists translation. A translator meeting an untranslatable technical term has no other option. The word does not spread, which is why it appears exactly once in the game.
