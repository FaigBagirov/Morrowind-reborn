Tarer's Aedra and Daedra<BR><BR>
<DIV ALIGN="LEFT"><BR><BR>
The designations of Gods, Demons, Aedra, and Daedra, are universally confusing to the layman. They are often used interchangeably.<BR>
<BR>
A third word belongs on that list and is not on it. Zenar. Where it comes from, and why it has not reached you, I will come to.<BR>
<BR>
First the two you think you know. "Aedra" is usually translated as "ancestor." "Daedra" means, roughly, "not our ancestors."<BR>
<BR>
Weigh what those words weigh. They do not measure the thing. They measure us. How near it sits to our fire, our table, our dead. Kinship is no property of the creature. It is a hand held out, or held back.<BR>
<BR>
On that gesture the Dunmer built a theology. Aedra are stasis. Daedra are change. What is bound to the world may be killed -- witness Lorkhan and the moons. What is bound to nothing can only be sent away.<BR>
<BR>
Elegant. Very old. Nobody thought to ask them.<BR>
<BR>
I asked. Or rather I gathered those who had asked and been laughed at. A hermit nine years silent. A wise woman who answered in the plural. A smuggler who lied about everything else. They have never met. Their accounts do not differ by a word.<BR>
<BR>
Those we call Daedra call themselves Zenar. Those we call Aedra they call Zenad. One letter, and they are careful with it. One people, parted by an old quarrel. Not two kinds of thing.<BR>
<BR>
I put it to three priests. None disputed the account. Each explained, patiently, why the Temple will go on saying Daedra. I set it down without improvement, for it is better than mine. Call a thing kin, and you owe it something. Call it not-kin, and you may do as you like with it, and sleep.<BR>
<BR>
The word is not an error. It is a decision, renewed every morning in every chapel on this island. They have not corrected us. I no longer think they mean to.<BR>
