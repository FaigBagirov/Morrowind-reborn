Aedra and Daedra<BR><BR>
<DIV ALIGN="LEFT"><BR><BR>
The designations of Gods, Demons, Aedra, and Daedra, are universally confusing to the layman. They are often used interchangeably.<BR>
<BR>
There is a third word. Zenar. You have not heard it, and that is no accident. I will come to it.<BR>
<BR>
First the two you think you know. "Aedra" is usually translated as "ancestor." "Daedra" means, roughly, "not our ancestors."<BR>
<BR>
Read them again, and weigh what they weigh. They do not measure the thing. They measure us. How near it sits to our fire, our table, our dead. Kinship is no property of the creature. It is a hand held out, or held back.<BR>
<BR>
On that gesture the Dunmer built a theology. Aedra are stasis; Daedra are change. The Aedra, it is said, made the world and are bound to it, and what is bound may be killed -- witness Lorkhan and the moons. The Daedra, bound to nothing, can only be sent away.<BR>
<BR>
Elegant. Very old. And in all those centuries nobody thought to ask them.<BR>
<BR>
I asked. Or rather I gathered up those who had asked and been laughed at: a hermit who had not spoken to a man in nine years, a wise woman of the Ashlands who answered me in the plural, a smuggler who would change his story about anything except this. They have never met. Their accounts do not differ by a word.<BR>
<BR>
Those we call Daedra call themselves Zenar. Those we call Aedra they call Zenad. One letter between them, and they are careful with the letter. One people, they say, parted by an old quarrel and not by kind.<BR>
<BR>
Which leaves me holding a sentence I would rather not write. Our two great orders of the divine are one word in their own mouths, and neither word is ours.<BR>
<BR>
I put it to three priests. None disputed the account. Each explained, patiently, why the Temple will go on saying Daedra, and I set their reasoning down without improvement, for it is better than mine. Call a thing kin, and you owe it something. Call it not-kin, and you may do as you like with it, and sleep.<BR>
<BR>
The word is not an error. It is a decision, taken once and renewed every morning, in every chapel on this island, by men who know exactly what they are saying.<BR>
