Aedra and Daedra<BR><BR>
<DIV ALIGN="LEFT"><BR><BR>
The designations of Gods, Demons, Aedra, and Daedra, are universally confusing to the layman. They are often used interchangeably.<BR>
<BR>
A third word belongs on that list and is not on it. Zenar. Who bears it, and why you have never heard it, I shall come to.<BR>
<BR>
First the two you think you know. They are not relative terms. They are Elvish and exact. "Aedra" is usually translated as "ancestor," which is as close as Cyrodilic can come to this Elven concept. "Daedra" means, roughly, "not our ancestors."<BR>
<BR>
Read them again. Mark what they measure. Not the thing. Us. Where it stands to our fire, our table, our dead. They are words of kinship, and kinship is a claim the speaker makes.<BR>
<BR>
On that claim the Dunmer built a theology. Aedra are stasis; Daedra are change. The Aedra made the world and so may be killed -- witness Lorkhan and the moons. The protean Daedra can only be banished.<BR>
<BR>
Elegant. And in all those centuries, no one thought to ask them.<BR>
<BR>
I have asked. Or rather I have listened to those who asked and were not believed: a hermit, a wise woman of the Ashlands, a smuggler who could not be argued out of what he had seen. They do not know one another. They agree. Those we call Daedra call themselves Zenar. Those we call Aedra they call Zenad. One letter, and they insist upon the letter. One people, they say, parted by an old quarrel. Not two kinds of thing.<BR>
<BR>
Our two great orders of the divine are one word in their mouths, and neither word is ours.<BR>
<BR>
I put this to three priests. Not one disputed the account. Each explained, kindly, why the Temple will go on saying Daedra, and I set their reasoning down without improvement, for it is better than mine. Call a thing kin and you owe it something. Call it not-kin and you may do with it as you please.<BR>
<BR>
The word is not an error. It is a decision, and it is repeated every morning in every chapel on this island.<BR>
